WHAT BIODEX SHIPS

`duke_ethnobot.json` beside this file is that table, reduced: one entry per
taxon, keyed by lower-cased "genus species", holding the eight most-cited
non-Poison activity names, the count of non-Poison records, and a flag saying
whether any record for the taxon is a Poison record. It is regenerated by
`tools/catalogue/build_catalogue.py` and committed, so the app's lookups work
offline.

CC0 is what makes bundling the file legitimate; USDA ARS is credited in the app
wherever a Duke's-derived fact is shown, and that credit is a courtesy rather
than a licence condition.

HOW BIODEX USES IT

A plant's "medicinal" tag is derived from this data — three or more distinct
non-Poison activities recorded for the species — and the entry shows the record
count and the activity names, attributed. Poison records are used at build time
as a checklist: a species Duke's records as poisonous must carry a "Caution:"
sentence in its curated note, or the catalogue build fails.

Duke's is a record of *traditional and documented uses*. It is not medical
advice, not a safety assessment, and BioDex does not present it as either.
